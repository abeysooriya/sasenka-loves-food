I don't usually review new openings, because most places need a few months to find their feet. Every now and then one is good enough from day one that I make an exception. @modobrisbane is one of them.

After launching in Phuket, MODO Pizza has landed in Brisbane, and a young crew and their family are running the show. The room is slick, but it hasn't lost that neighbourhood eatery feel. You could wander in on a Tuesday night or settle in for a long Saturday lunch.

Everyone loves a good pizza. I won't go as far as saying pineapple belongs on one, because it obviously doesn't. But stepping away from the traditional Italian pie is occasionally warranted. America doesn't do a lot well, but Italian culture runs deep there, and some of its pizza is epic.

Detroit-style was born in the 1940s. It's rectangular and baked in deep pans, with the cheese spread right to the edges so it caramelises into a crisp, lacy frico. The sauce is often spooned on top in “racing stripes”. The crust is thick, airy and chewy. So when MODO announced it was coming out hard with Detroit-style, I was very excited.

We started with the calamari fritti. It was golden, with a truffle mayonnaise on the side. Perfectly fine, nothing spectacular. The casa polpetta was lovely. Beef and pork meatballs braised in tomato sugo, finished with parmigiano and basil. Proper comfort. The bella burrata was great. Creamy Mount Tamborine burrata with sweet, jammy garlic confit cherry tomatoes. Simple and local, and it lets the ingredients do the talking.

Then the pizza. The Sunday Best comes with house vodka sauce, Italian fennel sausage, caramelised onion and Pecorino Romano. The crust is thick, airy and chewy, almost like focaccia, with a base that crackles when you bite in. Plenty of people leave their crust on a traditional pizza, but here the edge is the most memorable part of the whole thing. The cheese bakes right up against the pan into a dark, crunchy, almost toffee-like frill, and you'll fight over the corners. The vodka sauce is sweet and rich, with a gentle creaminess that softens the tomato. The cheese is just right, generous without drowning anything. The caramelised onion and a touch of mint work beautifully against the salty, aniseed hit of the fennel sausage. Every slice has the same care in it, right to the last corner.

The service has that easy, family-run warmth. The staff are genuinely excited about this place, and you feel it in every conversation, whether they're talking you through the menu or checking in on the table. Nothing feels scripted. This is a proper neighbourhood place.

Take this important lesson from me. Don't worry too much about the starters. They're lovely if you want to linger with friends, but get straight into the pizzas and order a couple of the very reasonably priced bottles of wine.

My excitement was warranted. I suspect MODO will become a favourite of mine, and I'm confident it will become one of yours too.
