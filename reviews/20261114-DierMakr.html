Second visit to Dier Makr, and I love this place. It's one of those places that feels like a secret. Tucked away and understated from the outside, but once you're in, it's like stepping into another world.

I put these guys firmly in the food geek category, and I mean that as the highest compliment. Chef-owner Kobi Ruzicka (@kobi_ruz) writes a new degustation every day around whatever Tasmania is offering. Apart from the salt and the olive oil, everything on the plate comes from the island, I was told. The cooking is innovative, but they still let the produce do most of the talking.

The menu is handed to you at the end, handwritten and sketched with peas and artichokes, which tells you a lot about the place.

We ordered champagne to get going, and with it came the milk bread. It's soft and pillowy, Japanese-style, with a flaky, buttery pull that's almost croissant. Lovage butter and black garlic sat alongside, sweet and deeply savoury. Yummy.

Then came the snacks: baby abalone with fennel, a cockle and cucumber ceviche with fig leaf, curd with beans, and a hare pâté en croûte. Clean and briny, then fresh and green, then rich and gamey to finish. They paired it with a sparkling beer. Weird on paper, very cool in the glass. The bitterness and fizz cut straight through the pâté.

White asparagus, savagnin and calamari came next. White asparagus is only in season for a very short time, so I felt lucky to be there for it. Tender and delicate, with that gentle sweetness that makes it so special. The sauce and the calamari made it a stunning dish.

The globe artichoke, oats and pork was a standout. The oats are their take on risotto, because oats grow in Tasmania and rice doesn't. Cooked al dente, still with a little crunch, nutty and creamy at the same time. Artichoke came two ways, with a dried, crisp version on top, all brought together by an earthy mushroom sauce. The pork cheek was excellent, soft enough to fall apart with a spoon, and beautiful against the bite of the oats.

Little Wool lamb a few ways. First, hidden in the spinach. Then a tartare, clean and silky. Then served straight with a sauce, and that one was EPIC. The garlic at the end was so good I'd happily have had a bowl of it on its own.

The palate cleanser here is always good. This time it was peach sorbet with bubbles: icy, fizzy, bright, and gone far too quickly.

To finish, a zucchini flower, aka the “choux thing”. I'm still not entirely sure what was going on, and I loved it.

The staff are all lovely, welcoming, and genuinely passionate about every course they put down. You can tell they believe in what they're serving, and that makes it all taste better.

The vibe is epic and the music is on point. Two visits in and Dier Makr still feels like Tasmania on a plate, cooked by people who care. I would come back for the oat risotto and that lamb alone.
